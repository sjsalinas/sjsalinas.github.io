About

From membranes to memory.

I graduated summa cum laude from Texas Tech University with a B.S. in Biochemistry and a minor in Biology. My research arc follows a single question across scales: how do cells withstand damage, and what happens when they cannot?

As an undergraduate in Dr. Peter Keyel’s lab, I investigated how the pathogen Leishmania major repairs membrane damage from pore-forming toxins – work that culminated in a co-authored publication in Toxins. Today I work as a Medical Research Technician III in Dr. Josh Lawrence’s lab at Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center, asking whether vitamin A and HDAC inhibition can stall or reverse decline in the J20 Alzheimer’s mouse model.

Research

Three questions, one thread: cellular survival under stress.

Research image placeholder – add assets/j20.jpg

Lawrence Lab, TTUHSC · May 2025 – Present

Can nutrition and epigenetics rescue the Alzheimer’s brain?

Question
Do vitamin A and HDAC inhibition, alone or combined, stall neurodegeneration in the J20 model?
Methods
Dietary and pharmacological interventions with qRT-PCR, Western blot, histology, confocal microscopy, behavior, and patch clamp.
Findings
ATRA and vorinostat show neuroprotective effects across physiology, excitability, behavior, and biochemistry; presented at SFN 2025.
Why it matters
A cheap, preventative lever against Alzheimer’s decline.
Research image placeholder – add assets/leishmania.jpg

Keyel Lab, Texas Tech · Nov 2022 – May 2025

How does a parasite survive having holes punched in it?

Question
How do membrane microdomains in Leishmania major shape susceptibility to pore-forming toxins?
Methods
LC50 assays for streptolysin O, toxin binding assays, flow cytometry, fluorescence microscopy, cell culture.
Findings
Lipophosphoglycan and 14-methylated sterols protect against SLO-mediated pore formation. Published in Toxins 2026; presented at two ASCB meetings.
Why it matters
Advances membrane repair biology with implications for host–pathogen interaction and wound repair.
Research image placeholder – add assets/nasa.jpg

NASA Space and Life Sciences Institute · May – Aug 2022

What does moondust do to lungs?

Question
How do lunar dust and simulants affect lung epithelial cells?
Methods
Fluorescence microscopy and chemical modeling software in Dr. Jeeverajan’s lab.
Findings
Showed how regolith irritates lung epithelial cells, and flagged ways to cut exposure.
Why it matters
Crew health for sustained lunar exploration.
